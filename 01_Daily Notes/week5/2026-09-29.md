#### Notes
I think 2 important metrics to discuss is purity and completeness.
Savont at the species level does not find any false positives (purity of 1), whereas wf-16s has a lower purity, and thus has finds false positives. The completeness on the other hand is a bit lower for Savont, it does not find all species in the ground truth. The completeness for WF-16s is 1, meaning it has found all species that the ground truth expected. I am curious to see if we can possibly lower the amount of missing species in Savont.

 After toying around with different Savont settings, i could not seem to increase the completeness. After searching through the asv mapping file i found an interesting tie:
 ```bash
final_asv_69_depth_429 100.00% g__Escherichia; -> Greengenes_unannotated final_asv_69_depth_429 100.00% g__Escherichia;s__albertii; -> Escherichia albertii
 ```
Savont's species-level misses are explained by unresolved ties between correct matching named-species references and incompletely-annotated genus-only records present in the Greengenes2 database itself. The 2 lines above (from asv_mappings.tsv) (ASV 69, ASV 71) shows the same ASV achieving 100% identity against both a named species and an unannotated genus-only record at the same time, with inconsistent resolution of the tie.

After a quick conversation with a [d4straub](https://github.com/d4straub) I learned that this is a conservative choice Savont makes. So it is intended.

I will rerun the wf-16s workflow again, this time with a new `--min_ref_coverage 90 -> 70`. This will lower the minimum coverage a sequence should have to be defined as classified. This might possibly increase the sum of abundance, but could also decrease the purity.
